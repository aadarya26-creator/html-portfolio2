The Best Movies According To Adhya

My Top three movies of all time

BAGHBAN

Baghban is a classic because it hits a deeply emotional chord that almost anyone can relate to.

At its core, the movie explores the vulnerability of aging parents and the painful reality of filial ingratitude.

Here is a short breakdown of the concept that makes it so powerful:

The Ultimate Sacrifice: Raj (Amitabh Bachchan) and Pooja (Hema Malini) dedicate their entire lives, finances, and love to raising their four sons, treating them as their ultimate investment for old age.

The Harsh Reality: Once Raj retires and his savings are gone, the sons view their parents not as a blessing, but as a financial burden and an inconvenience.
The Heartbreak: Instead of keeping them together, the children cruelly decide to separate the parents—forcing them to split their time between different houses like pieces of luggage.
The Triumph: The emotional core of the movie is how Raj channelizes his grief, loneliness, and heartbreak into writing a book (Baghban, meaning "The Gardener"), which becomes a massive success, allowing the parents to regain their independence and self-respect.

Why the Concept Resonates So Deeply?

The movie works so beautifully because it acts as a mirror to society. It contrasts unconditional parental love with the transactional nature of modern relationships.

The emotional peak isn't just the sadness of their separation; it's the satisfying validation when the parents reclaim their dignity and show that a parent's worth doesn't expire when they stop earning.

It teaches a powerful lesson about respect, family values, and the emotional debts we owe to the people who raised us.

SOORYAVANSHAM

The reason why i love this movie is:
1. Unconditional Respect in the Face of Rejection:
Even when his father disowns him, Heera never speaks a single word against him. His goal isn't to get "revenge" or prove his father wrong; his goal is simply to prove himself worthy of his father's name.
2. The Power of a Supportive Partner: The relationship between Heera and Radha is a huge part of the concept. Radha doesn't look down on Heera for being illiterate; instead, she becomes his strength, helping him navigate the business world while he supports her dream of becoming an IAS officer.
3. The Sweet Taste of Reconciliation: The emotional payoff of the movie is incredibly rewarding. It shows that hard work and integrity can bridge even the widest gaps, culminating in the beautiful moment where the strict father finally melts, realizes his son's true worth, and proudly accepts him back into the lineage.

3 IDIOT

The concept works flawlessly because it weaves deep life lessons into brilliant comedy:

1. "Chase Excellence, and Success Will Follow" The movie turns the traditional mindset on its head. Instead of studying just to pass an exam or secure a high-paying salary, Rancho proves that if you master your craft out of passion, wealth and success naturally come looking for you.

2. Education vs. Rote Learning (Ratta) Through the legendary character Chatur ("Silencer"), the movie exposes the flaw of the education system. Chatur memorizes words without understanding them just to top exams, while Rancho actually applies knowledge. The film brilliantly proves that a degree is just a piece of paper if it doesn't teach you how to think.

3. "All Izz Well" The iconic phrase isn't a magical solution to life's problems; it's a psychological tool to trick your heart. The movie teaches that when fear takes over, calming your mind allows you to tackle challenges rationally rather than panicking.

In the end, 3 Idiots isn't just about college life—it is a beautiful reminder to parents and students alike that a child's worth shouldn't be measured by a report card, and that life is meant to be lived, not raced through.
I can relate with this movie.
